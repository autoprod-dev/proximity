How it works
You enter the facts you have. Proximity weighs them — the mix changes by sport — and turns that into a win probability. In a race or a political market, each runner or candidate gets a share that adds to 100%, plus a place chance for racing.
  • Team and racket: NBA, AFL, cricket, football (soccer) and tennis. Tennis includes surface and per-surface records. Football is modelled as a two-way result (no draw).
  • Combat: boxing and MMA, including reach, height, stance or style, layoff and recent bouts.
  • Racing: horse and greyhound fields, using common Australian form-guide terms such as barrier or box, weight carried or weight change, track and distance record, and days since last run.
  • Political markets: example betting markets only, not a forecast of how people will vote.
Odds you type in are converted to an implied probability after the bookmaker's margin is removed. If the model and the market disagree by more than 5 percentage points, it flags “Line looks mispriced”. Example entries are labelled and are not real statistics. Real data is entered by hand.
